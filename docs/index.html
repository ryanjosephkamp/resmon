Literature surveillance that admits what it does not know

resmon watches 27 open-access scholarly repositories for the papers that match a researcher's criteria, compiles them into chronological reading reports, and tells the researcher when the watching itself has broken. It runs on the researcher's own machine: the corpus, the credentials and the reports never leave it.

What it does

Watches, on a schedule

Deep Dive and Deep Sweep runs on demand, and Automated Deep Sweeps on a schedule, over a catalog of sources each with its own rate limit and its own terms. A source that goes down degrades a sweep; it does not fail it.

Says which kind of fact it has

A field extracted from a document, a field matched against the corpus and a field guessed by a model are three different things, and resmon labels which is which. Where it cannot know something, it says so instead of rendering a plausible number.

Notices when it has stopped working

A watchdog reports on the monitoring rather than the literature: which sources answered, which returned nothing, and which have quietly stopped answering at all.

Keeps everything local

A SQLite corpus on the researcher's own disk, credentials in the operating system's keyring, and an optional backup to their own Google Drive. No account, no server.
