One app watching 26 open scholarly sources for the papers you follow.

resmon is a free desktop app. It searches arXiv, PubMed, OpenAlex, CrossRef, and 22 more on demand or on a schedule, writes what it finds into reading reports on your own computer, and records what every source did on every run.

Download resmon Install from source

Tested on macOS with Apple Silicon. Intel macOS, Windows, and Linux builds are made by the release workflow and have not yet been launched. Details

What every source did

4 runs · 10 sources

A quiet week and a broken search must never look the same. resmon records what every source did on every run, and says why a source returned nothing: it answered and had nothing, it could not be asked, or it never answered. This is the Source wall from the session in the tour, starting with its verdict, word for word.

What it does

11 parts

  1. Deep Dive and Deep Sweep

    One source in depth, or many in one run. Before a search runs, resmon says what each selected source will do with it: how it combines your keywords, whether it can honor your dates, whether it needs a key.

  2. Routines

    The same search on a schedule, said in words. A routine fires from an optional background service (launchd, a systemd user unit, or Task Scheduler). Without the service, the Routines page says that nothing will run them.

  3. Reports and a reading queue

    Every run writes a chronological report. Markdown, and PDF where LaTeX is installed, with the run’s log and search record. Save the papers worth reading, and export references as BibTeX, RIS, or CSV.

  4. Explorer

    Search everything resmon has collected. One local corpus across every run, filtered by source, category, author, and date.

  5. Source wall and Watchdog

    Whether the watching itself is intact. The Source wall marks what each source did on each run. The Watchdog reports sources that fail repeatedly, routines that are overdue, and departures from your own history.

  6. Watch profiles

    Follow a person, not only a topic. resmon matches papers to the people you watch and says, for each match, what it can show about it and what it cannot.

  7. Retractions and updates

    Papers that changed after you found them. On request, resmon checks Crossref’s Retraction Watch data and the preprint servers for retractions, expressions of concern, journal versions of preprints, and newer versions.

  8. AI summaries and Ask

    With your own key or a signed-in CLI. Paper summaries and an assistant, through your own provider key or through Claude Code or Codex if you already use them. Nothing is billed through resmon.

  9. Delivery and backup

    Reports where you read. Email, a folder your cloud drive syncs, a signed webhook, or an Atom feed. Back up the database to a folder or to your own Google Drive.

  10. Library and Evidence

    Your own PDFs, kept exactly. Keep your files as unchanging local copies, read them in the app, and keep exact passages with your notes.

  11. An MCP server

    resmon’s searches and corpus, for other tools. Tools that speak the Model Context Protocol can run routines and sweeps, search the corpus, and read search records.

The app’s own Tutorials tab walks through every page, and the guide goes further.

On your computer

No account
There is nothing to sign up for, and no resmon server to sign in to.
Your corpus
A SQLite database and a folder of reports on your own disk.
Your keys
Kept in your operating system’s keychain, and sent only to the service they belong to.
No telemetry
resmon sends no usage data. Summaries, delivery, and backup send only what you switch on, to where you point them.
