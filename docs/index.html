Research moves fast. resmon does, too.

resmon is a free desktop app. It asks arXiv, PubMed, OpenAlex, Crossref, and 21 more on a schedule or when you ask, keeps every paper it finds on your computer, and records what each source did on every run. This is its morning view, drawn from a real collection.

What every source did

A quiet morning and a broken search must never look the same. resmon records what every source did on every run, and says why a source returned nothing: it answered and had nothing, it could not be asked, or it never answered. What it does

Today
What arrived since you last marked all read, on a map of the collection. The map says whether it places papers by shared wording or by your embedding model. New papers are ringed and numbered, with the same numbers in the list beside it.
Watches
A search resmon runs on a schedule, keeping every paper it finds. A watch can follow words, a person’s new papers, or retractions and corrections on a person’s papers. The Calendar shows when each is due, and says so when nothing will run it.
Search
Search a collection, ask the sources, or find one paper. A Deep Dive asks one source in depth and a Deep Sweep asks several at once, with each source’s limits stated first. Every source is asked for each of your terms, and Match: Any Term or All Terms decides which papers are kept. Look Up a Paper looks up a DOI, arXiv id, PMID, or title and says how each copy matched. Export every collection match as BibTeX, RIS, or CSV.
Runs
Running now, waiting for a free slot, and the history. Watch each source’s progress, see why a run failed, and select runs to export or run again. The monitor in the top bar keeps progress and unseen failures in view.
Library
One gallery for your reading queue, files, and projects. Keep PDF, TXT, MD, and TeX files, read them in the app, and pin exact passages in Evidence. Get the PDF downloads copies only under a stated open license and the source and host rules. The reading queue works on Windows; kept files and Evidence do not.
Ask
An assistant that reads your watches, runs, and papers. Through Claude Code or Codex under your own sign-in, or your own provider key. It uses the same tools an outside program can, and nothing is billed through resmon.
Health
Whether the watching itself is intact. The Source Wall marks what every source did on each recent run. The Watchdog says what resmon can tell is wrong with its watching, and a check finds retractions and newer versions of papers you hold.
Outside the window
A command line, an MCP server, and a local HTTP API. The resmon command ships inside the app and installs from its menu. Tools that speak the Model Context Protocol can run watches and searches and read the collection.
Delivery and backup
Reports where you read them. Every run writes a report as a web page, Markdown, and a PDF where a LaTeX engine is installed, with its papers and search record. A watch can email it (Gmail and iCloud Mail settings are filled in for you), post it to a signed webhook, or put it in a folder or a feed on your own disk. Back up the database to a folder you keep.

Keep several collections in one database, open them in separate windows or macOS tabs, and arrange the sections in Settings › Top Bar. The guide covers each screen, and Settings › Tutorials has short recordings.

On your computer

No account
There is nothing to sign up for, and no resmon server to sign in to.
Collection
One database for your collections, watches, runs, and reading, with reports and Library files on your own disk.
Your keys
Kept in your operating system’s keychain, never in the database, and sent only to the service they belong to.
No telemetry
resmon sends no usage data. Searches go to the sources they ask; Ask, summaries, embeddings, PDF downloads, and delivery use the connections you choose. Backups are written to a folder you keep.
